More about this app

How it works

A scale is the set of pitch classes a piece draws on, numbered from the tonic as scale degrees. Moving the shape round the circle changes the tonic; changing the shape changes the mode. The chromagram's 12 bars are measured from the app's own output and folded into one octave, as the chapter describes.

In just intonation each degree sits at a small whole-number ratio to the tonic. The diatonic degrees take the 5-limit ratios 1/1, 9/8, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2, 5/3 and 15/8; the chromatic degrees take 16/15, 6/5, 45/32, 8/5 and 9/5, the same ratios as in the Interval lab. The frequency column is that of the degree in the octave above the tonic shown on the keyboard.

Things to try

Compare Lydian with major and listen for the one degree that differs. Step through the seven modes with the same tonic and watch which positions on the circle switch on and off. Play the major pentatonic over a drone and hear that no note clashes.

Keep the drone on, play the major scale in equal temperament and then in just intonation, and listen to the third: the just third locks with the drone, the equal-tempered one beats gently. Watch the chromagram while the drone plays: the tonic and its fifth carry most of the energy even though the melody visits every degree.
